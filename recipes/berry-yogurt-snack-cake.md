# Berry Yogurt Snack Cake

> The cake that makes dessert a reasonable breakfast: whole wheat flour, Greek yogurt keeping it moist for days, half the sugar of a typical snack cake, and two cups of berries folded through. One bowl, one pan, no frosting required.

## Ingredients

- 190 g / 1.5 cups white whole wheat flour
- 1.5 tsp baking powder
- 1/2 tsp baking soda
- 1/2 tsp fine salt
- 2 large eggs
- 100 g / 1/2 cup sugar, plus 1 tbsp turbinado or demerara for the top
- 250 g / 1 cup plain Greek yogurt (2% or whole)
- 60 ml / 1/4 cup olive oil or mild vegetable oil
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- zest of 1 lemon (optional)
- 250 g / 2 cups blueberries or raspberries (frozen work too — see note)
- 1 tbsp flour, for tossing the berries

## Instructions

1. Heat oven to 175°C / 350°F. Line a 23 cm / 9-inch square pan with parchment, leaving a sling over two sides.
2. In a large bowl, whisk the eggs and sugar until pale and slightly thickened, about 1 minute. Whisk in the yogurt, oil, vanilla, and lemon zest until smooth.
3. Add the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt and fold with a spatula just until the batter comes together — a few streaks of flour are fine; overmixing makes it tough.
4. Toss the berries with the 1 tbsp flour (this suspends them through the cake instead of sinking) and gently fold them in, leaving a few berries behind to press on top.
5. Scrape into the pan, smooth the top, scatter the reserved berries, and sprinkle with the turbinado.
6. Bake until golden and a tester in the center comes out with moist crumbs but no wet batter, 32–38 minutes. Tent with foil if the top browns before the center is done.
7. Cool in the pan 15 minutes, then lift out by the sling and cool fully before slicing — the crumb firms as it cools.

## Notes & Substitutions

- Frozen berries: don't thaw, and don't rinse; toss them in the flour while still frozen and fold gently to avoid purple batter. Frozen raspberries bleed more than blueberries.
- Swap the lemon zest for orange, or the berries for diced stone fruit in summer, or 1.5 cups of chocolate chips if you're being honest about it being dessert.
- Olive oil gives a faint fruity note; use neutral oil if you'd rather not taste it.
- The 2-cups-of-yogurt-to-little-fat ratio is the trick: the cake stays moist without a butter brick, for days.

## Storage & Make-Ahead

- Keeps 3 days at room temperature — it's actually moister on day two.
- Freezes beautifully: wrap individual squares and freeze up to 2 months; they thaw in a lunchbox by noon.
- Breakfast math: a square with Greek yogurt and coffee runs under 300 kcal total.
