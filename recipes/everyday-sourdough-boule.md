# Everyday Sourdough Boule

> A modest, repeatable sourdough: 30 minutes of actual work spread across a day, an overnight cold rest that makes the schedule forgiving, and a Dutch oven that does the steaming for you. Tangy, open-crumbed, and just 10% whole wheat so it stays tender enough for sandwiches.

## Ingredients

- 100 g active, bubbly sourdough starter (fed 4–12 hours earlier)
- 350 g / 1.5 cups lukewarm water
- 450 g / 3.6 cups bread flour
- 50 g / scant 1/2 cup whole wheat flour
- 10 g / 2 tsp fine sea salt
- rice flour or extra flour, for dusting

## Instructions

1. In a large bowl, stir the starter into the water until mostly dissolved. Add both flours and mix to a shaggy dough with no dry pockets. Cover and rest 30 minutes (autolyse — the flour hydrates and the gluten organizes itself).
2. Sprinkle the salt over the dough and squeeze and fold it in until the salt disappears and the dough feels smoother, about 1 minute.
3. Over the next 2–3 hours, do 4 sets of stretch and folds: grab one edge of the dough, stretch it up, and fold it over the center; rotate the bowl a quarter turn and repeat until you've gone all the way around. Cover between sets and space them 30–45 minutes apart.
4. Let the dough continue rising at room temperature until puffy, domed, and about 75% bigger, with bubbles visible at the edges — typically 4–6 hours total from mixing in a 21–24°C kitchen. Warm kitchens run faster; trust the dough, not the clock.
5. Shape: tip the dough onto a lightly floured counter and fold the four sides toward the center to build surface tension. Flip seam-side down, drag it in circles to tighten, then place seam-side up in a floured banneton or flour-dusted bowl lined with a tea towel. Cover.
6. Refrigerate 12–18 hours. The cold slows the ferment to build sour flavor and firms the dough for scoring.
7. When ready to bake, heat the oven to 250°C / 480°F with the Dutch oven and its lid inside for 45 minutes. Turn the chilled dough onto parchment, slash once deeply down the middle at a shallow angle, and lower it into the pot on the parchment.
8. Bake covered 20 minutes, then uncovered at 230°C / 450°F until deep brown, 20–25 minutes more. The loaf should read 98–99°C inside and sound hollow on the bottom.
9. Cool at least 1 hour before cutting — the crumb finishes setting as it cools, and steam trapped by early slicing gummy-textures the middle.

## Notes & Substitutions

- No starter yet: mix 60 g whole wheat flour + 60 g water in a jar and feed daily with equal parts flour and water; it typically takes 5–7 days to smell pleasantly sour and double between feedings. (Or ask a baking neighbor — starters divide happily.)
- One slash down the middle (a "bake-house ear") is the easiest strong score; any deeper pattern works on chilled dough.
- Push the whole wheat to 100 g for more tang and fiber; expect a slightly tighter crumb.
- No Dutch oven: bake on a preheated sheet pan with a roasting pan of hot water on the rack below for steam, removing it after 20 minutes.

## Storage & Make-Ahead

- Sourdough keeps 3–4 days cut-side-down on a board at room temperature.
- Slice and freeze up to 3 months; toast slices straight from the freezer — this is the single best way to always have good bread.
- Retarding the dough longer in the fridge (up to ~24 hours) deepens the sourness; shorter (8 hours) keeps it mild.
