# Labneh Breakfast Platter

> A Middle Eastern breakfast of thick, tangy labneh draped in olive oil and
> za'atar with crunchy vegetables — five minutes to assemble (or make real
> labneh overnight and taste the difference).

## Ingredients

- 500 g / 2 cups thick Greek yogurt (or store-bought labneh)
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 1 tsp lemon juice, if using yogurt
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, plus more to finish
- 2 tsp za'atar
- 1 English cucumber, halved lengthwise and sliced
- 250 g / 2 cups cherry tomatoes
- 4 radishes, thinly sliced
- a handful of kalamata olives
- fresh mint leaves
- warm pita or flatbread, to serve
- honey, optional for drizzling

## Instructions

1. Quick labneh: stir the salt and lemon into the yogurt. For true labneh,
   line a sieve with a clean towel, add the yogurt, and drain in the fridge
   12-24 hours — it thickens into a spreadable, tangier cheese.
2. Spread the labneh across a serving platter in a swoosh, making a shallow
   well in the center.
3. Drizzle generously with olive oil, dust with za'atar, and pile the
   cucumber, tomatoes, radishes, and olives around it.
4. Finish with mint, a pinch of flaky salt, and honey if you like the
   sweet-savory angle. Serve with warm pita for scooping.

## Notes & Substitutions

- The overnight drain is the whole point of labneh — try it once on a
  weekend; the quick version is a fine stand-in.
- Gluten-free: serve with GF flatbread or cucumber spears as scoops.
- Lighter swap: use 2% Greek yogurt (skip nonfat — it turns thin and sour).
- Savory upgrades: everything bagel seasoning, chili oil, or crushed
  pistachios instead of za'atar.

## Storage & Make-Ahead

- Drained labneh keeps 5 days refrigerated in a sealed container under a
  film of olive oil.
- Assembled plates are best eaten within the hour; components keep 3 days
  separately.
- Double-drained labneh can be rolled into balls and frozen 1 month.
