# Moqueca (Brazilian Fish Stew)

> From Bahia: tender fish poached in a coconut-tomato broth perfumed with
> peppers, scallions, and cilantro. One pot, twenty minutes of simmering,
> and dinner tastes like the Brazilian coast.

## Ingredients

- 800 g / 1.75 lb firm white fish (cod, haddock, or mahi), cut in 5 cm / 2 inch chunks
- 2 limes, juiced, plus wedges to serve
- 1 tsp salt, plus more to taste
- 2 tbsp dendê (red palm oil) or olive oil
- 1 large onion, halved and sliced thin
- 1 red bell pepper, sliced thin
- 1 yellow bell pepper, sliced thin
- 4 scallions, sliced, whites and greens separated
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 small hot pepper (habanero or bird's eye), left whole
- 400 g / 14 oz canned diced tomatoes
- 400 ml / 1.5 cups full-fat coconut milk (or light coconut milk for a leaner stew)
- 1 tsp paprika
- a large handful of cilantro and parsley, chopped
- steamed rice, to serve

## Instructions

1. Season the fish with the lime juice and salt; set aside while you build
   the base.
2. Heat the oil in a wide, deep skillet over medium; cook the onion,
   peppers, scallion whites, and garlic with a pinch of salt until
   softened but not browned, 6-7 minutes.
3. Add the tomatoes and paprika; simmer until saucy, 5 minutes. Pour in
   the coconut milk, add the whole hot pepper, and simmer 5 minutes more.
4. Nestle the fish into the broth in a single layer; spoon sauce over and
   simmer gently, uncovered and barely bubbling, until the fish just
   flakes, 8-10 minutes — don't stir, just baste.
5. Off the heat, remove the hot pepper, shower with scallion greens,
   cilantro, and parsley, and taste for salt. Serve over rice with lime.

## Notes & Substitutions

- The whole hot pepper perfumes the stew without detonating it — fish it
  out, and tell guests what it was.
- Dendê oil is traditional and gives the iconic color and flavor; it's
  high in saturated fat, so 2 tbsp is the moderate dose. Olive oil works.
- Shrimp moqueca: add peeled shrimp in the last 3-4 minutes.
- Lighter swap: light coconut milk saves ~80 kcal per serving; the stew
  stays silky.

## Storage & Make-Ahead

- Best the day it's made; keeps 2 days refrigerated — reheat gently and
  don't let it boil (the fish toughens).
- The tomato-coconut base can be made a day ahead; add the fish fresh.
- Not a freezer — cooked fish and coconut milk both suffer.
