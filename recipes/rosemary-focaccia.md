# Rosemary Focaccia

> The friendliest yeasted bread there is: a wet, no-knead dough, an overnight-or-same-day rise, and a hot pan of olive oil that fries the bottom into a golden crust while the top stays cloud-soft. Dimple it, stud it with rosemary, and watch people fight over the corner pieces.

## Ingredients

- 500 g / 4 cups bread flour (or 400 g bread flour + 100 g whole wheat)
- 2 tsp instant yeast
- 2 tsp fine sea salt
- 400 ml / 1.7 cups lukewarm water
- 4 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary, leaves stripped and roughly chopped
- 1 tsp flaky salt
- 150 g / 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved (optional)
- coarsely ground black pepper

## Instructions

1. In a large bowl, whisk the flour, yeast, and salt. Add the water and 2 tbsp of the olive oil and stir with a spatula or dough whisk for about 1 minute, until no dry flour remains. The dough will be shaggy and sticky — that's correct.
2. Cover and let rise at room temperature until puffy and doubled, 1–1.5 hours (or refrigerate overnight for deeper flavor; let it sit at room temperature 1 hour before continuing).
3. Pour the remaining 2 tbsp olive oil into a 23x33 cm / 9x13 pan and spread it up the sides. Scrape in the dough and turn it once to coat. Gently stretch it toward the corners; it will fight you — let it rest 15 minutes and stretch again.
4. Let rise uncovered until visibly puffed and jiggly, 40–50 minutes. Meanwhile, heat the oven to 230°C / 450°F.
5. Dimple the dough all over with oiled fingertips, pressing to the bottom of the pan without deflating it completely. Press the rosemary and tomatoes (if using) into the dimples, then sprinkle with the flaky salt and a grind of pepper.
6. Bake until deep golden on top and at the edges, 20–25 minutes. Run a spatula around and under the loaf and lift it onto a rack — cooling it in the pan traps steam and softens the bottom crust. Best eaten warm.

## Notes & Substitutions

- The olive oil in the pan is the whole point: it fries the base crisp. Don't skimp, and don't substitute a neutral oil — you'll taste the difference.
- Flour the toppings, not the dough: coarse salt, sliced olives, thinly sliced onion, or za'atar all work in place of rosemary and tomato.
- For a whole-grain loaf, keep the swap at 100 g whole wheat; more makes the crumb dense and the crust leathery.
- Instant yeast needs no proofing; if you only have active dry, stir it into the water first and wait 5 minutes until foamy.

## Storage & Make-Ahead

- Focaccia is best the day it's baked, but leftovers keep 2 days wrapped at room temperature — revive in a 200°C / 400°F oven for 5 minutes.
- Freeze cut pieces up to 2 months and reheat from frozen, straight into a hot oven.
- The overnight refrigerator rise (step 2) is the scheduling secret: mix it after dinner, dimple and bake it before lunch.
