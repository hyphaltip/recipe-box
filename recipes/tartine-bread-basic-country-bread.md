# Tartine Bread (Basic Country Bread)

> A classic San Francisco-style country sourdough with a crackling crust and open, custardy crumb — the foundational loaf from Tartine Bakery.

## Ingredients
- 700 g water, at 80°F (plus 50 g reserved)
- 200 g leaven (active, bubbly sourdough starter)
- 900 g white bread flour
- 100 g whole wheat flour
- 20 g fine sea salt

## Instructions
1. In a large bowl, dissolve 200 g leaven in 700 g water at 80°F, stirring until cloudy, 1–2 minutes.
2. Add 900 g white flour and 100 g whole wheat flour; mix with your hands until no dry flour remains and a shaggy dough forms, 2–3 minutes. Cover and rest (autolyse) 25–40 minutes.
3. Sprinkle 20 g salt over the dough and pour in the reserved 50 g water; squeeze and fold until the salt is fully dissolved and the dough smooths, 3–5 minutes.
4. Cover and bulk ferment at 75–80°F for 3–4 hours, performing 4 sets of stretch-and-folds at 30-minute intervals during the first 2 hours: wet your hands, grab one side of the dough, stretch it up and fold it over, then rotate the bowl a quarter turn and repeat until you've gone all the way around.
5. After the fourth set of folds, let the dough rest undisturbed until it is aerated, jiggly, and has risen about 20–30%, 1–2 more hours.
6. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and divide into two equal pieces, about 900 g each; shape each into a tight round and rest, covered, 20–30 minutes.
7. Shape each round into a final tight boule or batard; place seam-side up in floured proofing baskets, cover, and refrigerate 8–12 hours for the final proof.
8. The next morning, place a Dutch oven (or baking stone with a steam pan) in the oven and preheat to 500°F (260°C) for at least 45 minutes.
9. Turn one loaf out onto parchment, seam-side down, and score the top with a lame or sharp knife, 1/4 inch deep.
10. Transfer the loaf to the hot Dutch oven, cover, and bake at 500°F for 20 minutes; uncover, reduce to 450°F (230°C), and bake until deep golden brown and the internal temperature reaches 205–210°F (96–99°C), 20–25 more minutes.
11. Cool on a wire rack at least 1 hour before slicing; repeat with the second loaf.

## Notes & Substitutions
- The original image contained only the formula; the steps above follow the standard Tartine country bread method.
- The 50 g reserved water is added with the salt after the autolyse to help dissolve it and bring the dough to 75% hydration.
- As you get comfortable with softer dough, increase hydration gradually to 80–85% to suit your taste and get a more open crumb.
- For a 100% whole wheat loaf, replace all the white flour with whole wheat and increase hydration to 80–85%; expect a denser, more rustic bread.
- This bread is naturally vegan and dairy-free.
- If you don't have a leaven, build one by mixing 100 g flour and 100 g water with 20 g active starter and fermenting until bubbly, 8–12 hours at room temperature.

## Storage & Make-Ahead
- Shaped loaves can be cold-proofed in the fridge for up to 48 hours; longer proofing develops more flavor and a more open crumb.
- Baked bread keeps at room temperature, wrapped in a cloth or paper bag, for 2–3 days.
- Freeze sliced bread in an airtight bag for up to 3 months; reheat slices in a toaster or at 350°F (175°C) for 5–10 minutes.
