# Turkey and White Bean Chili with Dried Chiles

> Lean turkey and creamy white beans simmered in a smoky dried-chile purée — a
> warming chili whose flavor deepens overnight, making it a meal-prep champion.

## Ingredients

- 4 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 3 large or 6 medium ancho chiles, dried
- 2 dried guajillo chiles
- 2 dried pasilla chiles
- 2 canned chipotle chiles in adobo
- 5 tbsp neutral oil, divided (original: 6)
- 3 lb ground turkey, preferably dark meat
- kosher salt, to taste
- 2 onions, finely chopped
- 5 garlic cloves, smashed
- 1 tbsp ground cumin
- 2 tsp dried oregano
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 x 350 ml / 12 oz bottle hard cider or lager (or 1 cup extra broth)
- 1 x 7.5 cm / 3 inch cinnamon stick
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 x 440 g / 15.5 oz cans cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
- 3 tbsp pure maple syrup
- 1 cup plain Greek yogurt
- 2 tbsp fresh lime juice (from 1-2 limes)
- a handful of cilantro leaves and stems, finely chopped
- freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- tostadas or tortilla chips, to serve

## Instructions

1. Warm the broth in a large Dutch oven until steaming. Meanwhile, stem the
   ancho, guajillo, and pasilla chiles, tear the flesh into pieces, and discard
   the seeds. Steep the chile pieces in the hot broth until softened,
   25-30 minutes.
2. Add the soaked chiles, broth, and chipotles to a blender; blend until
   completely smooth, about 1 minute. Set the purée aside.
3. Heat 2 tbsp oil in the same pot over medium-high. Season the turkey with
   salt, add half of it, and press it flat against the pot to brown — cook
   undisturbed until deeply browned on the bottom, 8-10 minutes. Transfer to a
   plate and repeat with 2 tbsp more oil and the remaining turkey.
4. Heat the last tbsp of oil over medium; add the onions and garlic with a
   pinch of salt, cover, and cook until translucent and lightly browned at the
   edges, 10-12 minutes.
5. Stir in the cumin and oregano until fragrant, about 1 minute, then the
   tomato paste until darkened, about 1 minute more. Pour in the cider (or
   extra broth), scraping up the brown bits, and simmer until mostly reduced,
   about 5 minutes.
6. Add the chile purée, beans, maple syrup, and 4 cups water (swirl the
   blender with it to catch every bit of purée). Boil, then simmer partially
   covered, stirring occasionally, until thick and deeply flavored, about
   1 hour.
7. Return the turkey and any juices and simmer until the flavors meld,
   about 30 minutes longer.
8. While the chili finishes, stir the yogurt with the lime juice and cilantro;
   season with salt.
9. Season the chili with salt and pepper, then remove the cinnamon stick and
   bay leaves. Serve in bowls with a dollop of the yogurt sauce and broken
   tostadas.

## Notes & Substitutions

- Lighter swap: this version uses 5 tbsp oil instead of 6, and you can drop it
  to 4 — the browning still works at medium-high heat.
- Dairy-free: skip the yogurt sauce and serve with avocado and lime.
- Dried-chile shortcut: replace the soaked chiles and blending step with
  3 tbsp ancho chili powder whisked into the broth.
- Dark-meat turkey keeps it juicy; 85/15 ground beef works too (less lean).
- No cider or beer? Use 1 cup extra broth — add it with the purée in step 6.

## Storage & Make-Ahead

- Refrigerate up to 3 days (without toppings); freezes up to 3 months.
- The flavor genuinely improves overnight — a prime batch-cook candidate.
- Reheat gently on the stovetop, loosening with a splash of broth; make the
  yogurt sauce fresh.
