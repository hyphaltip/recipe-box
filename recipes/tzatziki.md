# Tzatziki (Greek Yogurt-Cucumber Sauce)

> The classic Greek cool-down: thick strained yogurt, salted cucumber,
> garlic, and dill. Five ingredients, fifteen minutes, endless uses.

## Ingredients

- 500 g / 2 cups Greek yogurt (5% or 2%, not nonfat)
- 1 English cucumber (about 350 g)
- 2 garlic cloves, grated to a paste
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 tbsp lemon juice or red wine vinegar
- 1 tsp fresh dill, chopped (or 1/2 tsp dried), plus more to finish
- 3/4 tsp salt, plus more for the cucumber
- fresh mint, optional

## Instructions

1. Grate the cucumber on the large holes of a box grater; toss with 1/2 tsp
   salt and let sit 10 minutes.
2. Squeeze the cucumber in a clean towel or fine sieve until quite dry —
   this is the difference between great tzatziki and yogurt soup.
3. Stir the cucumber into the yogurt with the garlic, olive oil, lemon,
   dill, and 1/4 tsp salt.
4. Chill at least 30 minutes (the garlic mellows); taste and adjust salt
   and lemon. Finish with a drizzle of olive oil and dill.

## Notes & Substitutions

- Serve with the [Chicken Souvlaki Bowls](chicken-souvlaki-bowls.md), as a
  veggie dip, on pita, or alongside grilled anything.
- Nonfat yogurt works but is tangier and thinner — strain it 1 hour first
  and expect a looser sauce.
- Grating the garlic to a paste (with a microplane or the flat of a knife
  and a pinch of salt) distributes the heat evenly — no harsh bites.
- Mint-for-dill is a lovely variation.

## Storage & Make-Ahead

- Refrigerate up to 4 days in an airtight container; stir before serving
  (a little weeping is normal).
- Do not freeze — it separates.
- Keeps 1 hour at room temperature max; serve chilled.
