Kung Pao Chicken

The Sichuan stir-fry that travels light: glossy chicken in a sweet-tart soy-black vinegar glaze, roasted peanuts, and the signature málà buzz of dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorn. Faster than delivery.

25 min total (15 prep + 10 cook)4 servingseasy difficultyChinese · dinner

Ingredients

  • 450 g / 1 lb boneless chicken thighs, cut in 1.5 cm / 1/2 inch cubes
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tsp Shaoxing wine (for the chicken marinade)
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp Chinese black vinegar (or balsamic), 2 tsp sugar, 1 tsp cornstarch, 2 tbsp water
  • 8-12 dried red chilies, snipped in half (shake out some seeds for milder heat)
  • 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns
  • 1.5 tbsp neutral oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tbsp ginger, julienned
  • 1 bell pepper, diced
  • 4 scallions, cut in 2 cm / 1 inch lengths
  • 45 g / 1/3 cup roasted unsalted peanuts

Instructions

  1. Toss the chicken with the marinade ingredients; let sit 10 minutes while you prep everything else (stir-fries wait for no one).
  2. Stir the sauce ingredients in a small bowl until the cornstarch dissolves.
  3. Heat the oil in a wok over the highest heat you can manage until shimmering. Add the dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns; stir 15 seconds until fragrant but not blackened.
  4. Add the chicken in a single layer; sear undisturbed 1 minute, then stir-fry until just cooked through, 2-3 minutes more.
  5. Add the garlic, ginger, and bell pepper; stir-fry 1 minute.
  6. Give the sauce a stir, pour it in, and toss until it glazes everything, about 1 minute. Off the heat, fold in the scallions and peanuts. Serve with steamed rice.

Notes & Substitutions

  • Málà — numbing-hot — comes from the peppercorns; toast them first in a dry pan for 30 seconds and grind coarsely for a stronger buzz.
  • Velvet-lite: for extra-tender chicken, blanch the marinated cubes in barely simmering water for 40 seconds before stir-frying (traditional velveting uses oil; water works nearly as well and lighter).
  • Vegetarian: swap in cubed firm tofu (pat very dry) or rehydrated mushrooms; use tamari to keep it gluten-free.
  • No black vinegar? Balsamic + a drop of rice vinegar approximates its smoky-sweet tang.

Storage & Make-Ahead

  • Best straight from the wok; keeps 2 days, reheat hot and fast.
  • The sauce can be mixed days ahead; prep components keep 2 days.
  • Peanuts go in fresh at reheating to stay crunchy.
