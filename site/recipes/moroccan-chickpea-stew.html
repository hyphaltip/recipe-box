Moroccan Chickpea & Sweet Potato Stew

A warming tagine-style stew: sweet potato, chickpeas, and apricots in a fragrant ras el hanout broth, finished with lemon and a swirl of harissa.

55 min total (15 prep + 40 cook)6 servingseasy difficultyMoroccan · lunch

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 3 carrots, cut in thick half-moons
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp ras el hanout (or 2 tsp cumin + 1 tsp ginger + 1/2 tsp cinnamon + 1/2 tsp turmeric)
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 800 g / 28 oz canned crushed tomatoes
  • 500 ml / 2 cups vegetable broth
  • 1 large sweet potato, about 450 g, cubed in 2.5 cm / 1 inch pieces
  • 2 x 400 g / 14 oz cans chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 60 g / 1/2 cup dried apricots, quartered, optional
  • 150 g / 5 oz kale or chard, stemmed and torn
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice, plus wedges to serve
  • salt and pepper, to taste
  • harissa and chopped cilantro, to serve
  • cooked couscous or rice, to serve (rice keeps it gluten-free)

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium; soften the onion and carrots with a pinch of salt until the onion is translucent, 6-8 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic, ras el hanout, and paprika; stir until fragrant, 1 minute.
  3. Add the tomatoes, broth, sweet potato, chickpeas, and apricots; bring to a simmer and cook uncovered until the sweet potato is tender and the stew has thickened, 25-30 minutes.
  4. Stir in the greens and cook until wilted, 3-4 minutes; finish with the lemon juice, salt, and pepper.
  5. Serve over couscous or rice with a swirl of harissa, cilantro, and lemon.

Notes & Substitutions

  • The apricots give the classic sweet-savory tagine note; raisins work too, or skip for a lower-sugar version.
  • Not-vegan upgrades: stir in shredded rotisserie chicken at the end, or crumble feta on top.
  • No ras el hanout? The substitute blend above gets you 90% there.
  • Squash or pumpkin swaps cleanly for the sweet potato.

Storage & Make-Ahead

  • Refrigerate up to 5 days; freezes up to 3 months (without the greens if you can — add them fresh when reheating).
  • Thickens as it sits; loosen with broth when reheating.
  • Serve over fresh-cooked grains so they don't soak up all the sauce.
